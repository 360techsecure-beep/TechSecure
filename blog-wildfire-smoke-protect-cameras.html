Wildfire & Smoke Season: Protecting Your Outdoor Cameras in LA

Every LA fire season brings the same risks for outdoor cameras: thick smoke, falling ash, brutal heat, and power cuts right when you most want to watch your property — sometimes from an evacuation zone miles away. A little preparation now keeps your system useful when conditions get bad.

Smoke and ash degrade your view

Heavy smoke cuts visibility for any camera, and settling ash coats the lens and housing, leaving footage hazy or washed out. During and after smoky stretches, wipe down lenses and domes gently — grinding dry ash across the glass will scratch it. Cameras tucked under an eave stay cleaner than fully exposed ones, which is worth considering when you place them.

Heat is the quiet killer

Fire season overlaps with LA's worst heat, and cheap cameras fail in it — sensors wash out, housings warp, and units drop offline in the afternoon. If yours already struggle in summer, fire season is when it shows. Our guide on how LA sun and heat affect outdoor cameras covers choosing gear that survives it.

Plan for the power going out

Utilities cut power during high fire risk (Public Safety Power Shutoffs), and that takes most camera systems down with it. If you want footage during an outage — exactly when you're away and worried — you need battery backup on the right parts of the system. We walk through this in will your cameras keep recording during a power outage.

Remote access is everything during an evacuation

If you have to leave, the ability to pull up your cameras from your phone is the whole point. Before the season peaks, confirm you can log in remotely, that alerts reach your phone, and that footage is saved somewhere you can still get to if the house loses power or connectivity — cloud backup earns its keep here. Weigh the options in cloud vs. local storage.

A quick pre-season checklist

  • Clean every lens and housing before the smoke arrives.
  • Confirm remote viewing and phone alerts actually work.
  • Add battery backup where you need footage during a shutoff.
  • Make sure recent footage is backed up off-site.
  • Replace any camera that already flakes out in summer heat.
