How to Stop False Motion Alerts on Your Security Cameras

A camera that cries wolf is almost as useless as no camera at all. When your phone buzzes forty times a day for passing cars, swaying branches, and shifting shadows, you stop looking — and that's exactly when you miss the one alert that mattered. The good news: false alerts are almost always fixable. Here's how.

1. Set activity zones

Most modern systems let you draw "zones" so the camera only cares about certain areas. Box in your porch, walkway, and driveway; exclude the public sidewalk and the busy street beyond it. This single change eliminates the biggest source of junk alerts — traffic you can't do anything about anyway.

2. Turn on person and vehicle detection

Older cameras trigger on any pixel change: headlights, rain, a cat, a flag. Newer AI-based cameras can tell a person or vehicle from a moving branch and only alert on what matters. If your camera supports it, switch to person, package, and vehicle detection — it's the single biggest upgrade to alert quality. Our rundown of the features that actually matter covers which ones do this well.

3. Adjust sensitivity — carefully

Cranking sensitivity to the max feels safer but buries you in noise. Dial it down until routine movement stops triggering it, then test by walking through the frame yourself. You want it to fire on a person at the door, not a moth on the lens.

4. Fix what's physically in the frame

  • Trim the branch that sweeps across the view on windy afternoons.
  • Re-aim the camera so a busy road or a neighbor's driveway isn't dominating the shot.
  • Watch your lighting. Harsh shadows and headlight glare cause phantom motion — steady security lighting helps the camera see cleanly.

5. Mind night-time triggers

Infrared night vision attracts bugs and spiders right up to the lens, and their movement sets off alerts all night. Cleaning the lens and adding a little ambient light near the camera helps a lot — more on that in our night vision guide.

Still buried in alerts?

Sometimes the fix is placement or hardware — a camera pointed at the wrong thing, or a model too old for smart detection. If you've tuned everything and your phone still won't stop buzzing, we can re-aim, reconfigure, or upgrade the cameras causing it. The goal is simple: alerts you actually trust. We'll get you there.
